All you need to know about Transition to kVAh Billing Across Maharashtra

A significant change has been made to the calculation of electricity bills. This change, mandated by the Maharashtra Electricity Regulatory Commission (MERC) as of March 29, 2025, impacts all commercial and industrial consumers with a sanctioned load above 20 kW.

What’s Changing?

  • New Billing Method: Your electricity bill will now be based on kVAh (apparent energy) instead of just kWh (active energy).
  • Why kVAh?- kVAh billing includes both active energy (used for work) and reactive energy (supports voltage but doesn’t do useful work). This means your bill will reflect total energy usage more accurately.

Why This Change?

  • Promotes Efficiency: kVAh billing encourages efficient energy use by discouraging the excessive consumption of reactive power.
  • Benefits: Improved energy efficiency, reduced network losses, better reliability, and a more sustainable energy system.

    • What You Should Do

    Install Capacitors or APFC Panels

    Upgrading or installing capacitor banks or Automatic Power Factor Correction (APFC) systems can enhance your Power Factor, leading to reduced kVAh consumption and lower electricity costs.

    Review Tariff Options

    If your sanction load requirement is under 20 kW, reach out to your utility provider to discuss the possibility of switching to a tariff plan that better suits your needs.

    Switch Off Capacitors When Not in Use – Achieve Unity Power Factor with Minimal Cost Involvement

    If your system has a lead power factor (PF) capacitor, you can achieve nearly unity power factor (PF = 1) by turning off the capacitor when not needed, especially at night. While capacitors help improve power factor for inductive loads like motors, they can cause overcompensation if left on continuously. By switching off the capacitor during non-operating hours, you can enhance efficiency and reduce unnecessary energy use without any added operational costs.

    Capacitors Should Be Switched on According to Requirement – Make the Most of Your Investment

    To maximize your investment in capacitors for power factor correction, switch them on and off based on your system's actual needs. Activate when there’s a significant load on your system that demands power factor correction improving your system’s efficiency and reducing energy costs. The right balance ensures that you're getting the best return on your investment.

    APFC Panels Should Be Configured Correctly for 3-Phase Supply

    Automatic Power Factor Correction (APFC) panels are essential tools for maintaining an optimal power factor by dynamically adjusting capacitor banks according to the load. However, a common issue encounter is the installation of APFC panels on single-phase systems, despite the fact that the supply is a three-phase system. This misconfiguration can result in inadequate power factor correction and may even damage equipment. It is crucial for APFC panels to be properly configured for a three-phase supply to ensure that each phase is balanced, allowing the system to achieve its full potential in power factor correction. If you suspect that your APFC panel is incorrectly set up, it's advisable to consult a professional to correct the configuration based on your specific power supply needs.

    APFC not working or is not being maintained

    Issues such as faulty calibration, aging components, or an APFC that isn't functioning properly or is poorly maintained can have a significant impact on your system's efficiency. By regularly monitoring your power factor and making the necessary adjustments, you can ensure that your system operates at optimal efficiency. Even minor corrections can result in substantial improvements in energy consumption.

    In conclusion, transitioning to kVAh billing is a key step in improving energy efficiency for commercial and industrial consumers. By maintaining a power factor close to unity through optimal capacitor use, proper APFC panel configuration, and consistent monitoring, businesses can effectively manage electricity costs.
