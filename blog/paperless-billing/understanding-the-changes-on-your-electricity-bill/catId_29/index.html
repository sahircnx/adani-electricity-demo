FAC Explained: Understanding the Changes on Your Electricity Bill


Have you ever looked at your electricity bill and wondered why the cost sometimes rises unexpectedly? One possible reason might be the Fuel Adjustment Charge (FAC). Let's break down what this means and how it might affect you.

What is a Fuel Adjustment Charge in Electricity Bill (FAC)?

Think of the FAC as a way to balance the scales when the cost of fuels like coal or natural gas (used to generate electricity) changes or when power costs change in response to the demand and supply gap on Power Exchanges (like share market prices). When electricity prices are first set, there's an estimate for how much those fuels will cost and at what rate power will be available on Power Exchanges. If the actual price goes much higher, the FAC kicks in to help cover the difference. It can also work in reverse—if the prices drop, you might see a credit on your bill.

Why does my bill seem higher in the summer?

  • · Hot weather = more air conditioners running to maintain the same level of cooling or increased period of usage = higher electricity demand.
  • · When many people need electricity simultaneously, the increased need is met through increased generation or procuring it from Power Exchanges on a Short-Term basis.
  • · Increasing generation requires procurement of additional fuel at the market rate, increasing the cost of electricity generation.
  • · Increased demand for Power Exchanges with the same available resources increases prices based on the demand-supply gap.
  • · To meet summer demand, increased electricity requirement has to be bought at short-term rates; this summer (April to June), rates are as high as Rs 7-10 per unit! That's just the energy cost; then there is the cost of delivering that power to your home.
  • · This is why the FAC has recovered to help cover these unexpected costs.
  • · Utilities work hard to minimize the impact of the FAC on your bill. However, in high fuel or power purchase costs, the FAC could become a more significant portion (caped 20%). Check your bill statement for the details.

Who decides on the FAC charges in the electricity bill?

Not the utility company! A Quasi-Judicial Regulatory Authority called the State Electricity Regulatory Commission (SERC) reviews fuel costs and approves the FAC if needed. Key Things to Remember

  • · FAC is not permanent: It adjusts based on the current price of fuels or power rate on Power Exchange, so it might go away when prices/demand stabilize.
  • · FAC is about fairness: It helps ensure everyone pays their share when electricity procurement costs rise unexpectedly.
  • · You have some control: Conserving electricity, especially during hot weather, can lower the impact of additional consumption and FAC.

We're in this together. Even with things like the FAC, your utility company works hard to keep things affordable. Most of your power comes from steady, predictable sources. Those occasional spikes in energy demand/cost can lead to changes in your bill. Imagine FAC like grocery shopping on a budget.

  • · You plan your meals and estimate how much each ingredient will cost (similar to how electricity prices are set with an estimated fuel cost/Power Exchange rates).
  • · When you go shopping, the price of things like vegetables or meat might be higher than expected (similar to how fuel costs/Power Exchange rates fluctuate).

In this situation, you have two options:

  • · Reduce your overall grocery spending: You might buy less or find cheaper alternatives (like using less electricity during peak hours or using energy-efficient appliances).
  • · Pay a little extra: You cover the difference between the planned budget and the actual cost (similar to the FAC on your electricity bill). Here's the key takeaway:
  • · The FAC isn't an extra charge the utility company adds for fun. It's a way to adjust for unexpected changes in fuel costs/Power Exchange rates, just like you might need to adjust your grocery budget if prices increase.

Additional points to consider:

  • · You can't control the price of groceries, but you can control how much you buy (similarly, you can't control fuel costs but your electricity usage).
  • · Just like you wouldn't expect your grocery bill to stay precisely the same every week.
